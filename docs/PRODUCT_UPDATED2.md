# Faida: expanding the product beyond opportunity discovery

The opportunity here is bigger than a WhatsApp agent that helps Kenyans find and apply for benefits. Faida could become a conversational service-delivery and programme-management platform connecting individuals, organisations and the institutions responsible for delivering services.

Your existing product concept already establishes the foundation:

- For individuals: discover relevant opportunities, understand what they qualify for, complete applications and track progress.
- For organisations: reach the right people, receive complete applications and improve programme uptake.
- For both: make institutional services accessible through familiar, low-friction communication.

The natural next step is to extend Faida into the ongoing administration of programmes, particularly for NGOs, foundations, county governments and development partners.

The key is to preserve the core identity: Faida makes the first move, helps people take action, and follows through until the outcome.

## 1. The full Faida use-case map

The use cases fall into six connected product areas. They share the same underlying capabilities: a consented profile, an opportunity and programme catalogue, conversational messaging, workflow automation, document handling and case tracking.

### Programme delivery platform

### C. Natural, accessible feedback and accountability

Feedback should be a core interaction, not a separate survey link people rarely open.

## Example: a participant's feedback journey

1. After a training session

   Faida asks: "How was today's training? You can reply in your own words or send a voice note."
2. When something went wrong

   "Did you have any difficulty getting here, receiving your materials or being treated fairly?"
3. When a concern is raised

   Faida records the concern, explains what happens next and provides a reference number.
4. During follow-up

   The participant can ask for an update, add information or request that a sensitive issue be handled privately.
5. When resolved

   Faida informs the participant of the outcome and asks whether the issue has been addressed.

Feedback capabilities could include:

- Text, voice notes, structured questions and assisted responses in accessible languages.
- Anonymous feedback where feasible, with clear explanation of its limitations.
- Private grievance reporting with restricted access and escalation rules.
- Safeguarding and fraud reports routed to authorised staff, not general programme administrators.
- Two-way status updates, acknowledgements and resolution tracking.
- Community consultations, public participation and beneficiary-led suggestions.
- Satisfaction and outcome check-ins at relevant moments rather than repetitive surveys.

Important design principle: feedback must not affect eligibility or access to services. Participants should know who can see their responses, how their information will be used and how to raise concerns outside Faida if necessary.

### D. Organisation and institutional engagement

Faida can also help organisations maintain ongoing relationships with the people they serve.

- Member services: SACCO dividends, loan eligibility, insurance renewals and member notices.
- Employer services: recruitment, onboarding, training, employee benefits and compliance reminders.
- County services: public participation, bursary administration, permit renewals and local service announcements.
- Education: scholarship applications, student onboarding, attendance and progression.
- Health: screening reminders, appointment coordination, treatment follow-ups and health campaign participation.
- Agriculture: farmer registration, seasonal inputs, extension services and crop insurance.
- Housing and utilities: application intake, eligibility checks, service appointments and payment reminders.

### E. Field operations and last-mile delivery

For programmes that still depend on physical delivery, Faida could coordinate the digital and field components:

- Notify participants of distribution dates, locations and required documents.
- Schedule appointments and manage attendance.
- Give field officers participant lists and case-specific instructions.
- Record delivery confirmations and unresolved issues.
- Support offline data capture and synchronisation for areas with limited connectivity.
- Escalate missed distributions, failed referrals and incomplete service delivery.

### F. Programme intelligence and learning

Once programmes use Faida throughout their lifecycle, it can help organisations understand not just how many people applied, but where delivery is failing.

Examples of useful intelligence:

- Which eligible communities are not being reached?
- Where do applicants abandon the process?
- Which documents or requirements create the most friction?
- Are selected participants actually receiving the promised service?
- Which complaints remain unresolved?
- Which interventions lead to better outcomes?

These insights could support adaptive programme management, resource allocation and evidence-based reporting to funders.

## 2. What this means for the product strategy

The expanded vision suggests three connected offerings rather than one enormous product.

Faida for people

A personal agent that finds opportunities, handles paperwork and keeps people informed.

Faida for organisations

A distribution and intake platform for reaching the right people and receiving genuine applications.

Faida for programmes

A programme administration layer for enrolment, case management, service delivery, feedback, monitoring and reporting.

The strategic advantage is that these offerings reinforce each other. An organisation lists a programme once; Faida reaches the right people; individuals apply through their agent; the organisation administers the programme; and Faida follows up on delivery and outcomes.
