# Faida — Opportunity Matrix

> **Status:** Draft v0.1 · October 2026 · companion to [`PRODUCT.md`](PRODUCT.md)
> **Question:** what is the full range of things Faida should capture, match and
> take up for people, and who is offering them?
> **Method:** desk research (Kenyan news, government and county sites, October
> 2026) plus the product doc. Figures are as reported and flagged where sources
> conflict. Sources are listed at the end.

---

## 1. The reframe

The landing page's "What Faida handles" is a list of named programmes. However
long it gets, a list reads as closed. It names what we have catalogued, not what
Faida is for.

What Faida is for is broader:

> **Anything offered to the public, by anyone offering it, brought to the people
> it fits, and taken up on their behalf.**

That is a two-sided exchange:

- **Supply:** every organisation that pushes something out to people: national
  government, counties and city councils, wards, constituencies, state agencies,
  development partners, NGOs, foundations, SACCOs, co-operatives, employers,
  colleges, health campaigns, utilities, even civic processes.
- **Demand:** every person, matched on who they are and what is happening in their
  life, not on what they think to search for.
- **Faida in the middle:** captures the offer, verifies it, matches it, tells the
  person first, does the application or claim, and follows it to the end. Then it
  reports uptake back to the organisation.

The matrix below maps that space along four axes: **who offers** (§2), **what
is offered** (§3), **what life moment makes it relevant** (§4) and **how uptake
physically happens** (§5). §6 has the findings that change the product.

**The finding in this table:** the supply is huge, scattered across hundreds of
publishers, and much of it, especially S3, S4 and S6, **never gets a proper web
page**. Faida needs more than scrapers. It needs a way for any organisation (and
any ward administrator) to list an offer in minutes, plus verification.

## 3. What is offered (offer types)

| # | Offer type | Examples | Typical window | What Faida does | Main barrier today |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| O1 | **Cash transfers** | Inua Jamii (70+, orphans, severe disability), relief cash | Registration drives, not continuous | Alert when a drive opens nearby; prepare; tell them what to bring | Nobody hears about the drive |
| O2 | **Grants** | NYOTA business grants, county youth and women grants, foundation grants | Weeks | Match, apply, follow vetting | Short window, USSD or web only |
| O3 | **Loans and credit** | Hustler Fund, YEDF, Uwezo, WEF, SACCO loans, HELB | Rolling or termly | Compare, apply, track repayment dates | Choosing safely; predatory lenders |
| O4 | **Subsidies and inputs** | Subsidised fertiliser through farmer registration, county seed and input schemes | Seasonal | Register in time for the season, remind at distribution | Registration is a prerequisite few know about |
| O5 | **Bursaries and scholarships** | NG-CDF, county, HELB, foundation scholarships | Days to weeks, often in January | Alert parents, fill forms, list documents, chase results | Paper forms, local-only notices |
| O6 | **Training, apprenticeships, internships** | NYOTA training, TVET intakes, Ajira, PSC internships | Intake dates | Match, apply, remind about attendance | Not knowing; costs of getting there |
| O7 | **Jobs** | Climate WorX, county jobs, public service, overseas jobs via NEA | Days (Climate WorX registration ran ~2 weeks) | Alert, verify the offer is genuine, apply | **Fraud**: fake KDF, police and TSC adverts |
| O8 | **Tenders and procurement** | AGPO 30% set-aside, county tenders, e-GP | Per tender | Keep the business registered and compliant, match tenders, prepare bids | e-GP registration, compliance, proxy capture |
| O9 | **Health cover and services** | SHA, screening drives, maternity, disability fund | Ongoing plus campaign days | Register, keep contributions current, alert for campaigns | Contributions lapse silently |
| O10 | **Housing and land** | Boma Yangu, county schemes, title issuance | Rounds | Register interest, track allocation | Long processes |
| O11 | **Legal help** | Legal aid, succession, ID and document disputes | When needed | Refer, book, prepare documents | Cost, intimidation |
| O12 | **Money that is already yours** | UFAA unclaimed assets (Ksh 65–82B held), NSSF benefits, refunds, insurance payouts, estates | No deadline | Search, prepare the claim, chase it | People do not know it exists |
| O13 | **Registrations that unlock other things** | ID, KRA PIN, NCPWD card, farmer registration, business registration, e-GP supplier registration | Ongoing | Do it early, because they gate O2–O9 | Unseen dependencies |
| O14 | **Member benefits** | SACCO dividends, welfare, co-op bonuses, employer benefits | Annual or event-based | Remind, claim, explain | Members do not read the circulars |
| O15 | **Insurance and protection** | Micro, crop and livestock insurance | Seasonal | Explain plainly, enrol, help with claims | Trust, complexity |
| O16 | **Utilities and connections** | Power and water connections | Rounds | Apply, track | Paperwork, site visits |
| O17 | **Civic voice** | Budget public participation, voter registration | Fixed dates | Tell people when and where; take a written submission | Low turnout among youth, women and PWDs |
| O18 | **Prizes and accelerators** | Innovation challenges | Weeks | Match, help draft, submit | Writing, deadlines |
| O19 | **Obligations** (the other half) | KRA returns, permits, licences, contributions | Fixed dates | File within limits; keep the person in good standing, which also keeps them eligible for O2, O3 and O8 | Penalties; lost eligibility |

## 4. Life moments (what makes Faida reach out)

Proactive matching needs triggers. Most of them are life events Faida can know
about from the profile or ask about once.

| Moment | What it opens up |
|---|---|
| Turning 18 | ID, voter registration, KRA PIN, youth funds, NYOTA (18–29) |
| KCSE results or a school transition | KUCCPS, HELB, scholarships, NG-CDF and county bursaries (e.g. Grade 10) |
| Out of work | Climate WorX, NYOTA training, NSSF, overseas jobs (verified only) |
| Starting a business | Business registration, permit, KRA, e-GP and AGPO, Hustler, YEDF, grants |
| Pregnancy and birth | SHA maternity cover, birth certificate |
| Disability | NCPWD registration → tax exemption, AGPO PWD category, disability fund |
| Turning 70 | Inua Jamii |
| A death in the family | Succession, NSSF survivor benefits, UFAA claims, insurance |
| Planting season | Farmer registration, subsidised inputs, crop insurance |
| Drought or floods | Relief registration, cash transfers |
| Moving county | A whole new set of county programmes |
| Joining a SACCO or employer | Member and staff benefits |
| Every year | Obligations calendar (O19) |

**Profile fields this implies:** age, county, ward, gender, disability,
education level and stage, occupation, business status and registrations,
farming (crops, land), household (dependants, older relatives), memberships
(SACCO, co-op, employer), documents held, language and channel.

## 5. How uptake physically happens

This decides what "Faida takes it up for you" means for each offer.

| Uptake channel | Example | What Faida can do |
|---|---|---|
| **Partner API or enrolment feed** | SACCO or partner programmes | Submit directly (best) |
| **Web portal** | eCitizen, HELB, KUCCPS, SHA, county e-permits, e-GP | Browser agent submits with consent; relays OTPs; keeps evidence |
| **USSD-only** | NYOTA `*254#`; UFAA has had a USSD check | Faida cannot dial from the user's line. It guides step by step on the call, or partners with the operator or programme for a direct route |
| **Paper form at an office** | NG-CDF and county bursaries at ward centres | Pre-fill a printable form, list documents, name the office and hours, remind before the deadline |
| **In-person drive or event** | Inua Jamii registration, health camps, public participation | Alert with date, venue and what to bring; remind the day before; record attendance |
| **Through an intermediary** | Co-ops, chiefs, employers | Hand over a complete, checked application to the intermediary |

The execution ladder in `PRODUCT.md` §7 (API → browser agent → guided
self-service) holds, but it needs two more rungs: **USSD guidance** and
**offline preparation** (forms, venues, documents). For bursaries and cash
transfers these are the main path, not edge cases.

## 6. Findings that change the product

1. **Timing is the product.** Windows are short and local: a 6-day NG-CDF
   bursary window, about two weeks for Climate WorX registration, Inua Jamii
   drives that come and go. Someone who hears late misses out. Proactive alerts
   aren't a nice-to-have. They are the reason Faida exists.

2. **Most supply is offline or local.** Ward bursaries, chiefs' barazas, county
   PDFs and Facebook posts never reach a database. Faida needs an
   **offer-listing desk** that any organisation, county officer or ward
   administrator can use in minutes (WhatsApp included), plus field partners and
   scraping with human approval.

3. **Unlock chains.** Many offers are gated by earlier registrations. AGPO needs
   a registered business, tax compliance and e-GP registration. The disability
   tax exemption needs an NCPWD card. Subsidised fertiliser needs farmer
   registration. Faida should plan these routes: "You are two steps from
   qualifying for X. Shall I start step one?" This is also where obligations and
   benefits meet.

4. **Verification is a feature, for both sides.** Fake KDF, police and TSC
   recruitment scams are common, and the government has deregistered 600+
   overseas recruitment agencies. People need to ask "Is this real?" and get a
   straight answer. Organisations need to know applicants really are in the
   target group: AGPO has been captured by proxy companies. A **verified offer**
   and a **verified applicant** are both things Faida can sell.

5. **"Money already yours" is the most surprising category.** UFAA reportedly
   holds Ksh 65–82B, and claims are a trickle. There's no deadline, the
   paperwork is heavy, and nobody knows to look. It's a clear, explainable
   win and a strong story for marketing.

6. **Civic opportunities belong in scope.** County budget forums are
   opportunities too, and turnout is skewed (one county: youth 28%, women 22%,
   PWDs 0.5% of participants). Telling people when and where, and taking a
   written submission, fits Faida's model and is valuable to counties.

7. **Uptake doesn't end at "approved".** Trainees must attend, grantees must
   report, borrowers must repay, beneficiaries must stay registered. Faida
   keeping people on track raises completion rates, which is the number
   programmes care about and would pay for.

8. **There's a line between an opportunity and an advert.** Once SACCOs,
   banks and insurers can push offers, Faida risks becoming an ad network.
   Proposed rule: an offer must give the person something (money, a service,
   a place, a job, a right) on transparent terms. Commercial products come only
   from regulated providers, are always labelled, and are ranked by benefit to
   the user, never by payment. This extends the "fair by design" line already
   on the landing page.

9. **The catalogue can't be hand-curated.** Today's ~30 curated benefits is
   the right start, but the target is thousands of live offers with windows.
   That needs one **offer schema** that every source maps into:

   | Field | Meaning |
   |---|---|
   | Offered by | Organisation, type (S1–S15), verification status |
   | What | Offer type (O1–O19), plain-language summary in EN and SW |
   | Who qualifies | Machine-readable rules (age, county, ward, gender, disability, education, business, farming, membership, income) plus a free-text note |
   | What's needed | Documents and prerequisites (links to other offers, for unlock chains) |
   | Window | Opens, closes, rolling, or event date and venue |
   | How to take it up | API, portal, USSD, paper, in person, intermediary |
   | Capacity | Slots (e.g. 70 per ward for NYOTA), so Faida can say "act now" honestly |
   | After uptake | Follow-on obligations (attend, report, repay) |
   | Source and expiry | Where it came from, when it was last checked |

## 7. What this means for the landing page

Replace the named-programme list with the shape of the exchange:

- **Lead line:** something like "If it's on offer, Faida knows about it."
  Then: "Grants, jobs, training, cover, places, payouts, and money that's
  already yours. From national government, your county and ward, NGOs,
  SACCOs, employers and colleges. Matched to you, and taken up for you."
- **Two compact lists instead of one long one:** *What* (the offer types in
  §3, in plain words) and *From whom* (the sources in §2). Named programmes
  appear only as small examples under each, so the section reads open-ended.
- **One line for organisations:** "Offering something to people? List it once.
  Faida finds who it fits." It links to the cobalt section.
- **Optionally, in the day story:** swap one moment for "money already yours"
  (UFAA) or a "Is this job real?" check. Both show range and trust.

## 8. Open questions

1. **Listing:** free for public bodies and NGOs, paid for commercial partners?
   Who approves a listing, and how fast?
2. **Verification:** what makes an organisation "verified"? Registration
   documents, a known official contact, a government partnership?
3. **Liability:** if a third-party offer turns out to be bad, what does Faida
   owe the user?
4. **USSD-only programmes:** partner for a direct route, or guide only?
5. **Offline supply:** do we recruit ward-level contributors (paid, or as a
   civic role) to list local offers?
6. **Commercial boundary:** which product categories are in, and which are out
   (e.g. no consumer credit outside regulated lenders)?
