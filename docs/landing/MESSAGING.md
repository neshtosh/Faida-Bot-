# Messaging map: from the opportunity matrix to the page

> **Status:** v1 · 5 November 2026
> **What this is:** the opportunity matrix turned into messages for each audience
> Faida sells to. Every message on the landing page should trace back to a row
> here, and every row here traces back to the matrix.
> **Inputs:** [`OPPORTUNITY-MATRIX.md`](../OPPORTUNITY-MATRIX.md) (§2 sources,
> §3 offer types, §4 life moments, §5 uptake channels, §6 findings),
> [`PRODUCT.md`](../PRODUCT.md), [`COPY-REVIEW.md`](COPY-REVIEW.md),
> [`.agents/product-marketing.md`](../../.agents/product-marketing.md).
> **Method:** the `marketingskills` copywriting skill (the Human Action Model of

## 1. One need, three audiences

The matrix's reframe (§1) is a two-sided exchange: organisations offer things,
people need them, and the two don't meet in time. That single gap gives three
audiences one shared need:

| Audience | Who they are (matrix) | The need, in their words | What Faida promises them |
|---|---|---|---|
| **People** | The demand side: anyone, matched on who they are and what's happening in their life (§4) | "I didn't know it existed until it closed." "I don't know if this is real." "The forms are too much." | Hear first, know it's real, have it done with your yes |
| **Organisations** | The supply side, S1–S15: national programmes, agencies, counties, wards, NGOs, foundations, SACCOs, co-ops, employers, colleges, health campaigns, utilities | "Our people never hear about it in time." "Applications come in incomplete, or from the wrong people." "They drop out after approval." | Your offer reaches the people it's for, and complete, genuine applications come back |
| **Organisations' own people** | The same people, seen from the organisation's side: its residents, members, beneficiaries, applicants and trainees | "I'm a member, but I never read the circulars." "I qualified but heard on the last day." | They hear about *your* offer under your name, in their language, and get help finishing it |

The third row is what ties the page together. The person in the day story
(Wanjiru) is the end user of every organisation in the cobalt chapter. Her 6:40
message starts as a listing from a county. So the page shows each organisation
what its own people receive, not just what Faida does.

## 2. People: the need comes from life moments

The matrix's §4 is the strongest copy source on the page. People don't think in
offer types ("O5 bursaries"); they think in what's happening at home. Each
moment opens offers (§3) that usually have a window, and many sit behind a
registration that has to be done first (§6.3, unlock chains).

| Life moment (§4), in the reader's words | What it opens (§3) | The cost of not knowing (§3 "main barrier", §6) | Message |
|---|---|---|---|
| Turning 18 | ID, KRA PIN, voter registration, youth funds, NYOTA (18–29) | The registrations gate everything after them | "Turning 18 opens more than an ID." |
| School results, Grade 10 or university | KUCCPS, HELB, scholarships, NG-CDF and county bursaries | Bursary windows are days long and on paper at the ward office | "The bursary forms are out. Here's what to bring, and it closes Friday." |
| Looking for work | Climate WorX, NYOTA training, internships, overseas jobs via NEA | Fake KDF, police and TSC adverts; short registration windows | "Real jobs, checked. Fake ones, named." |
| Starting or running a business | Permit, KRA, business registration, Hustler, YEDF, grants, AGPO tenders | Missed renewals cost fines and lose eligibility; AGPO needs registration and compliance first | "You're two steps from the tenders set aside for youth. Shall I start step one?" |
| A baby on the way | SHA maternity cover, birth certificate | Cover lapses quietly when contributions stop | "Your SHA cover is ready for the birth." |
| Living with a disability | NCPWD card → tax exemption, AGPO PWD category, disability fund | The card is the key few know about | "One card opens a tax exemption and a share of tenders." |
| A parent turning 70 | Inua Jamii | Registration drives come and go; nobody hears | "Cucu has turned 70. She now qualifies for Inua Jamii." |
| Planting season | Farmer registration, subsidised fertiliser, crop and livestock insurance | Registration is a prerequisite few know about | "Register before the season, or miss the fertiliser." |
| A death in the family | Succession, NSSF survivor benefits, UFAA claims, insurance | Paperwork is heavy; money sits unclaimed with no deadline | "Money left in old accounts can be claimed." |
| Drought or floods | Relief registration, cash transfers | Drives are local and short | "Relief registration is at the chief's office on Thursday." |
| Moving county | A new county's bursaries, funds and jobs | You don't know the new county's programmes | "New county, new programmes." |
| Every June | KRA return (O19) | Penalties; tax compliance gates tenders and some loans | "Your return is due by 30 June. It's ready to file." |

**What this proves:** most households have at least one of these moments in a
year. The page should let the reader see that for themselves (a checklist
of moments) instead of claiming it with an unsourced statistic.

**The four facts that make it essential, and their sources** (matrix Sources;
check each against the official source before publishing):

1. **Timing decides who gets it.** NG-CDF bursary window of 6 days in Teso North
   (Sh5,000 each); Climate WorX registration about two weeks; Inua Jamii drives.
   *(§6.1 "Timing is the product.")*
2. **Fraud targets the people who need it most.** KDF, the National Police
   Service and TSC have all issued public warnings about fake recruitment;
   600+ overseas recruitment agencies deregistered. *(§6.4)*
3. **Money already yours goes unclaimed.** UFAA reportedly holds Ksh 65–82B
   (sources conflict). *(§6.5)*
4. **Uptake is hard even when you know.** Portals behind logins and OTPs,
   USSD-only programmes, paper forms at ward offices. *(§5)*

**How Faida takes it up, by channel** (matrix §5, translated into promises a
person understands):

| Channel | Page language |
|---|---|
| Partner API / portal | "Faida applies for you when you say yes." |
| USSD-only | "Faida walks you through the code, step by step." |
| Paper form at an office | "Faida fills in the form for you to print, and tells you where to take it." |
| In-person drive | "Faida tells you where, when and what to bring, and reminds you the day before." |
| Through an intermediary | "Faida hands your complete application to your co-op, chief or employer." |

## 3. Organisations: the need comes from what goes wrong today

The matrix's §2 has, for every source type, "how people take it up today". That
column is each organisation's pain. Grouped into six buyer groups, each with its
own end users:

| Buyer group (§2) | Their people | What goes wrong today (§2, §6) | What Faida does | The number they care about |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Counties, wards and constituencies** (S3, S4, S14) | Residents, parents, farmers, traders | Bursary forms at ward offices with short windows; notices as PDFs, Facebook posts and notice boards; public participation dominated by the same few voices; proxy applicants | Tells eligible residents who opted in; pre-fills the form; names the office and the closing date; reminds them; tells residents when and where the budget forum is and takes written views | Eligible residents reached in time; complete applications; a fair spread of who applied |
| **National programmes and agencies** (S1, S2, S5) | Youth, older people, job seekers, taxpayers | Registration windows of days or weeks; places capped per ward (NYOTA: 70); USSD-only applications; mass drives; capture by proxies | Alerts the day a window opens; guides USSD and portal steps; checks applicants against the rules; keeps them on track after approval | Uptake by the target group; completion (attendance, reports, repayments); clean reporting to funders |
| **SACCOs, co-operatives, banks and insurers** (S8, S9) | Members and policyholders | Members don't read circulars; branch visits cost money; insurance is hard to explain | Tells each member about the dividend, bonus, loan or cover that fits them, under your name; brings back complete applications; ranks by benefit to the member | Member uptake; completed applications; retention |
| **NGOs, faith groups, foundations and prizes** (S6, S7, S15) | Beneficiaries, scholars, entrepreneurs | Support reaches people already in your network; field registration is costly; applications with essays and documents stall | Reaches eligible people outside your network; collects documents; follows through to completion | Reach beyond the usual network; completion; an outcome report for funders |
| **Employers, colleges and training providers** (S10, S11) | Applicants, students, trainees, interns | Fake adverts in your name; floods of unqualified applicants; no-shows | Checks adverts people forward against your official channels; sends real openings to people who meet your requirements; reminds trainees to attend | Qualified applicants; fewer scams in your name; attendance |
| **Health campaigns, utilities and housing** (S12, S13) | Patients, households, home-seekers | People don't hear about free screening until it's over; long, paper-heavy processes | Tells people in range when, where and what to bring, and reminds them the day before; tracks long applications | Turnout on the day; completed applications |

**Shared promise to every organisation** (matrix §6.2, §6.4, §6.7, §6.8):

1. **List it once.** By form, on WhatsApp or by connecting your system, in
   minutes. This includes a ward officer with a paper notice. *(§6.2: most
   supply is offline.)*
2. **The right people hear first.** Only people who meet the rules and chose to
   hear, in their language, with your name on it. *(§6.1)*
3. **Genuine, complete applications.** Checked against your rules, documents
   attached, sent to you or entered into your system. *(§6.4: a verified
   applicant is something Faida can sell.)*
4. **Followed through.** After approval Faida keeps people on track: training
   days, reports, repayments. *(§6.7: completion is the number programmes pay
   for.)*
5. **Fair by design.** People choose to hear from you, every offer is labelled,
   offers are ranked by what they give the person, and nobody can pay to skip
   eligibility rules. *(§6.8: an opportunity, never an advert.)*

**Objections to answer for organisations:**

| Objection | Answer | Status |
|---|---|---|
| "Will this flood us with applications?" | Only people who meet your rules are told, and each application is checked before it reaches you | Copy ready |
| "How do we know applicants are real?" | Faida checks each applicant against your rules and the documents they share, and flags what doesn't match. You make the final decision | Needs the verification process |
| "Is it an ad network?" | Every offer gives the person something on clear terms; commercial products are labelled and never ranked by payment | Policy needs adopting in writing |
| "What does it cost?" | `[NEED: B2B pricing. The proposal has white-label fees, listing fees and per-enrolment fees]` | Open |
| "Who else uses it?" | `[NEED: pilot partners]` | Open |

## 4. Message hierarchy for the page

1. **Hero (both audiences, consumer-first):** "Everything on offer. Faida brings
   you yours." The subhead names concrete offers from §3 and the three promises
   (hear first, it's real, done with your yes). A quiet link to organisations.
2. **The gap (people's discomfort):** three scenes from §3's barriers (the
   bursary window, the fake advert, the cyber café), and the turn.
3. **Life moments (the essential need):** §4 as a checklist the reader
   recognises, ending with timing and unlock chains.
4. **The day (vision):** five moments, each Faida-initiated, across offer types
   and obligations.
5. **You stay in charge (anxiety):** consent levels and promises.
6. **What Faida finds (range and trust):** §3 offer types and §2 sources, and an
   "is this real?" example (§6.4).
7. **FAQ:** people's objections, including "is this the government?".
8. **Organisations (the other side of the counter):** the shared gap, a listing
   that becomes Wanjiru's message (their people's view), the six buyer groups,
   the three steps, fair by design.
9. **Close:** two lines, one for each audience.

## 5. Words

| Use | Avoid on people-facing copy |
|---|---|
| bursary, fund, loan, grant, job, place, cover, payment, "money that's yours" | "offers" alone, "programmes", "benefits" as a category word |
| apply, register, claim, file, renew | take up, uptake, enrol |
| closes Friday, the window, the deadline | time-bound, rolling |
| you qualify | eligible, meets the criteria |
| people who chose to hear from you | opted-in users |
| members, residents, applicants, trainees (on organisation copy) | beneficiaries (except for NGOs), leads, users |
